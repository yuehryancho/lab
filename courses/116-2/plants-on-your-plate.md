## Course description

Every meal is a plant biology lesson. Each week we take one plant from the Taiwanese table, rice, soybean, tea, chili, banana, cooking oil, and ask what it is, how it grows, why it tastes the way it does, and how biotechnology is changing it. No prior biology is needed. The course runs alongside Plant Biology and gives you the stories behind the mechanisms you learn there.

## Format

Each 50-minute session: 10 minutes to look at, handle, and taste the plant of the week; 25 minutes of mini-lecture; 15 minutes of group discussion or hands-on activity.

## Weekly plan (16 weeks)

| Wk | Plant | Biology concept | Food and biotech link |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rice | What is a plant? Seed, embryo, endosperm, starch | Brown vs white rice; rice breeding in Taiwan |
| 2 | Soybean | Protein and oil in seeds; root nodules and nitrogen fixation | Tofu and soy milk; the GM soybean debate |
| 3 | Sweet potato leaves, spinach | Leaves and photosynthesis; chlorophyll | Iron and oxalate in spinach; nitrate in leafy greens |
| 4 | Tomato | What a fruit is; ethylene and ripening | Lycopene; Flavr Savr, the first GM food; genome-edited high-GABA tomato |
| 5 | Potato, sweet potato | Tubers and storage roots: how plants store energy | Solanine in green potatoes; the Irish famine and late blight |
| 6 | Banana | Clonal propagation and monoculture | Cavendish and Panama disease TR4; seedless fruit |
| 7 | Tea | Secondary metabolites: caffeine, catechins; why plants make toxins | Oolong "fermentation" is oxidation; polyphenol oxidase |
| 8 | Chili and pepper | Capsaicin and pain receptors; defense and seed dispersal | Scoville scale; capsaicin in medicine |
| 9 | **Midterm show-and-tell** | Each student brings one plant food and explains its biology in 1 minute | — |
| 10 | Wheat and bread | Gluten proteins; hexaploid wheat | Celiac disease; Green Revolution dwarfing genes |
| 11 | Maize | C4 photosynthesis; domestication from teosinte | Bt maize; high-fructose corn syrup; nixtamalization and niacin |
| 12 | Cooking oils: soybean, olive, camellia | Plant lipids: fatty acid composition, saturated and unsaturated | Oil bodies; high-oleic oils by breeding and engineering |
| 13 | Citrus, guava | Vitamins plants make and humans cannot | Vitamin C; Golden Rice and vitamin A biofortification |
| 14 | Coffee and cacao | Seed fermentation and roasting; the Maillard reaction | Climate change and coffee |
| 15 | Future foods | Plant-based meat: heme from soybean leghemoglobin; vertical farming | Genome-editing regulation in Taiwan; careers in plant biotechnology |
| 16 | **Final 3-minute talks** | "The plant on my plate": one plant, from cell to table | — |
