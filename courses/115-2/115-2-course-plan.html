2. 縱向設計：一條從細胞到分子的階梯

三門課加上 115-1 已開的兩門，構成 BFN 學程裡一條完整的植物／細胞生物學路徑：

大一下   Plant Biology       整株植物：細胞、組織、器官、生理、生殖、多樣性、應用
   ↓
大二下   Organelle Biology   拆開細胞：各胞器的結構、生合成、蛋白質運送、胞器溝通
   ↓
大三下   Plant Lipids        深入分子：脂質合成、膜、儲存、訊號、分析方法、油脂生技
   ↓
大三／四上  Introduction of Protein Homeostasis（115-1, BFN5013）
研究所      Plant Physiology and Stress Responses（115-1, BIOT7035）

設計原則：

  • 各年級一個尺度：大一談整株植物與器官，大二談胞器，大三談分子。同一主題（例如葉綠體、內質網）在三門課都出現，但深度逐級加深，避免重複講同一張投影片。
  • 每門課都連回學程主軸：BFN 是「生物科技＋食品營養」，每門課每週至少一個食品／生技的例子（例如大一的「盤子裡的植物」、大二的粒線體與營養代謝、大三的食用油脂肪酸組成）。
  • 每門課都連回你的研究：ER 恆定（大二第 5 週）、脂質調控 ER 壓力（大三第 13 週）、醣基化與蛋白質品管（大二第 5–6 週）。這些週的投影片可直接從實驗室 seminar 與 115-1 教材改寫。
  • 第一輪開課的特殊狀況：115-2 的大二、大三學生沒有修過前一階的課（例如大三修植物脂質的人沒上過胞器生物學）。第一年每門課的前 1–2 週要補基礎；從 116 學年起才會是完整的階梯。

  • 期中回饋：第 8 週做 5 分鐘匿名問卷，第 9 週回應。

Plant Biology 植物生物學

115-2 · Course code TBD · 3 credits (assumed) · Required/Elective TBD · Mon 6–8 (13:20–16:20) · Taught in English
Bachelor Program of Biotechnology and Food Nutrition (BFN) · Year 1 · Instructor: Yueh Cho

Course description

Plants feed us, clothe us, and run the planet's carbon cycle. This course gives first-year students a working map of how plants are built and how they work: from the plant cell and its unique organelles, through tissues and organs, to water and nutrient transport, photosynthesis, hormones, environmental sensing, reproduction, and evolutionary diversity. Every week links a core concept to a plant on your plate or a tool in the biotechnology industry. The course prepares you for Organelle Biology (year 2), Plant Lipids (year 3), and the plant-related electives of the BFN program.

Each week combines two lecture periods with one "studio" period for hands-on observation, problem solving, and short quizzes.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, you will be able to:

  1. Describe the structure of the plant cell, the major tissue types, and the anatomy of roots, stems, leaves, flowers, seeds, and fruits.
  2. Explain how plants move water, minerals, and sugars, and how they fix carbon in C3, C4, and CAM photosynthesis.
  3. Outline how hormones and environmental signals (light, gravity, day length) control plant growth and development.
  4. Trace the life cycle of a flowering plant from pollination to germination.
  5. Place the major plant lineages on a phylogenetic tree and relate their features to life on land.
  6. Connect plant biology to crop improvement, plant-based foods, and biotechnology, and read a simple plant science figure critically.

Prerequisites

General Biology (b)(1). Students take General Biology (b)(2) concurrently. No prior botany is assumed.

Textbooks and readings

  • Required (free, online): OpenStax, Biology 2e (2018). Chapters 8 (Photosynthesis), 25 (Seedless Plants), 26 (Seed Plants), 30 (Plant Form and Physiology), 31 (Soil and Plant Nutrition), 32 (Plant Reproduction). https://openstax.org/details/books/biology-2e
  • Reference: Evert RF, Eichhorn SE. Raven Biology of Plants, 8th ed. W.H. Freeman, 2013.
  • Reference: Mauseth JD. Botany: An Introduction to Plant Biology, 7th ed. Jones & Bartlett, 2021.
  • Reference (advanced): Taiz L, Møller IM, Murphy A, Zeiger E. Plant Physiology and Development, 7th ed. Oxford University Press, 2022.

Weekly reading assignments are posted on NTU COOL one week ahead.

設計理由與備課備忘（中文，自用）

定位

  • 這是學程裡植物相關課程的入口，對象是大一下、以國際生為主、同時修普通生物學（乙）二。課程要做的是建立「整株植物」的圖像，不是植物生理的進階版。深度對標 OpenStax／Raven，而不是 Taiz。
  • 每週三節：兩節講課、一節 studio。studio 是讓大一學生動手與互動的機會，也是全英語課堂裡讓程度較弱的學生跟上的緩衝。第一年若負荷過重，studio 可退化為小考＋習題，不影響架構。

進度安排的考量

  • 週一只有 14 次上課（3/1、4/5 停課，前者待確認）。期中排在第 9 週（4/19），期末在第 16 週（6/7），各佔兩節，第三節用來檢討或總結。
  • 順序：細胞 → 組織 → 器官 → 運輸 → 營養 → 光合（期中）→ 荷爾蒙 → 環境感知 → 生殖 → 多樣性 → 逆境與互動 → 應用。多樣性放到第 13 週而不是開頭，理由是先讓學生熟悉被子植物的結構，再談演化比較容易理解「哪些構造是陸生化的適應」。
  • 第 5、6、8、10、14 週可直接降階改寫 115-1 植物生理與逆境反應（BIOT7035）的投影片。

Studio 需要的材料與場地

  • 第 3、4、12 週需要新鮮植物材料（芹菜、青蔥、葉片、花）、放大鏡或 Foldscope、指甲油與透明膠帶（氣孔印模）。請確認教室是否允許，若是固定座位的階梯教室，就改為示範影像＋學習單。
  • 第 15 週海報：若修課人數超過 40 人，改為線上海報（NTU COOL 討論區）加 3 分鐘口頭簡介。

Organelle Biology 胞器生物學

115-2 · Course code TBD · 2 credits (assumed) · Required/Elective TBD · Fri 6–7 (13:20–15:10) · Taught in English
Bachelor Program of Biotechnology and Food Nutrition (BFN) · Year 2 · Instructor: Yueh Cho

Course description

A eukaryotic cell is a city of compartments. This course takes each organelle in turn and asks four questions: how is it built, how do proteins and lipids get there, what does it do, and what happens when it fails? We cover the nucleus, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, endosomes and lysosomes or vacuoles, mitochondria, chloroplasts and other plastids, peroxisomes, and lipid droplets, and finish with how organelles talk to each other and how biotechnology exploits them. Examples come from yeast, animal, and plant cells, with links to nutrition, disease, and food science throughout.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, you will be able to:

  1. Describe the structure, composition, and main functions of each major organelle.
  2. Explain the logic of protein sorting: signal sequences, translocation machineries, and vesicle traffic.
  3. Compare the endosymbiotic organelles (mitochondria and plastids) with the endomembrane system in origin, genome, and import mechanism.
  4. Relate organelle quality control (ER stress, autophagy, mitophagy) to cell survival and disease.
  5. Interpret fluorescence and electron micrographs, fractionation data, and simple organelle proteomics.
  6. Give examples of organelle engineering in biotechnology and of organelle dysfunction in human nutrition and disease.

Prerequisites

General Biology (b)(1)(2). Concurrent or prior biochemistry is helpful but not required; the course reviews membrane structure in Wk 2.

Textbooks and readings

  • Required: Alberts B, Hopkin K, Johnson A, Morgan D, Raff M, Roberts K, Walter P. Essential Cell Biology, 5th ed. W.W. Norton, 2019. Chapters 11 (Membrane Structure), 12 (Transport Across Cell Membranes), 14 (Energy Generation in Mitochondria and Chloroplasts), 15 (Intracellular Compartments and Protein Transport), 17 (Cytoskeleton).
  • Reference (advanced): Alberts B et al. Molecular Biology of the Cell, 7th ed. W.W. Norton, 2022. Chapters 10, 12, 13, 14, 16.
  • Selected review articles posted on NTU COOL for Wk 5, 9, 12, 14, and 15.

Plant Lipids 植物脂質

115-2 · Course code TBD · 2 credits (assumed) · Required/Elective TBD · Fri 8–9 (15:30–17:20) · Taught in English
Bachelor Program of Biotechnology and Food Nutrition (BFN) · Year 3 · Instructor: Yueh Cho

Course description

Lipids make up every membrane in a plant, store the energy in every seed, waterproof every leaf, and carry signals that decide whether a plant grows, defends itself, or dies. This course follows plant lipids from fatty acid synthesis in the plastid to the oils in your kitchen. We cover the biosynthesis of membrane glycerolipids, sphingolipids, and sterols; storage lipids and oil bodies; surface lipids; lipid signaling in development, stress, and immunity; the methods of modern lipidomics; and the engineering of plant oils for food, nutrition, and industry.

The primary literature for the course is built around one research program: the work of Yuki Nakamura's Plant Lipid Research Team (RIKEN CSRS, formerly Academia Sinica), which over two decades traced how Arabidopsis makes, remodels, and uses its glycerolipids. Following one lab's papers lets you watch a research question evolve from the first phosphate-starvation phospholipase (2005) to lipid-regulated flowering (2014) and ER homeostasis (2024). Students read primary literature each week and present one research paper in the final weeks.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, you will be able to:

  1. Draw the major plant lipid classes and explain where in the cell each is made.
  2. Trace fatty acid synthesis, desaturation, and glycerolipid assembly through the prokaryotic and eukaryotic pathways, and explain how lipids move between the plastid and the ER.
  3. Explain how membrane lipid composition changes with temperature, phosphate supply, and stress, and why.
  4. Describe triacylglycerol synthesis, oil body formation, and lipid mobilization in seeds.
  5. Outline the main lipid signaling pathways (phosphatidic acid, phosphoinositides, oxylipins, sphingolipids) and their roles in stress and immunity.
  6. Read and critique a lipidomics dataset, and evaluate a plant oil engineering strategy for food or industrial use.
