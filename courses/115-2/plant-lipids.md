## Course description

Lipids make up every membrane in a plant, store the energy in every seed, waterproof every leaf, and carry signals that decide whether a plant grows, defends itself, or dies. This course follows plant lipids from fatty acid synthesis in the plastid to the oils in your kitchen. We cover the biosynthesis of membrane glycerolipids, sphingolipids, and sterols; storage lipids and oil bodies; surface lipids; lipid signaling in development, stress, and immunity; the methods of modern lipidomics; and the engineering of plant oils for food, nutrition, and industry.

The primary literature for the course is built around one research program: the work of Yuki Nakamura's Plant Lipid Research Team (RIKEN CSRS, formerly Academia Sinica), which over two decades traced how Arabidopsis makes, remodels, and uses its glycerolipids. Following one lab's papers lets you watch a research question evolve from the first phosphate-starvation phospholipase (2005) to lipid-regulated flowering (2014) and ER homeostasis (2024). Students read primary literature each week and present one research paper in the final weeks.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, you will be able to:

1. Draw the major plant lipid classes and explain where in the cell each is made.
2. Trace fatty acid synthesis, desaturation, and glycerolipid assembly through the prokaryotic and eukaryotic pathways, and explain how lipids move between the plastid and the ER.
3. Explain how membrane lipid composition changes with temperature, phosphate supply, and stress, and why.
4. Describe triacylglycerol synthesis, oil body formation, and lipid mobilization in seeds.
5. Outline the main lipid signaling pathways (phosphatidic acid, phosphoinositides, oxylipins, sphingolipids) and their roles in stress and immunity.
6. Read and critique a lipidomics dataset, and evaluate a plant oil engineering strategy for food or industrial use.

## Prerequisites

General Biology and Biochemistry. Organelle Biology or Plant Physiology is helpful; Wk 1–2 review the membrane and organelle background needed.

## Textbooks and readings

- **Required chapters:** Buchanan BB, Gruissem W, Jones RL (eds). *Biochemistry & Molecular Biology of Plants*, 2nd ed. Wiley-Blackwell, 2015. Chapter 8 (Lipids).
- **Required review (open access):** Li-Beisson Y, Shorrosh B, Beisson F, et al. Acyl-lipid metabolism. *The Arabidopsis Book* 11: e0161 (2013).
- **Reference:** Heldt H-W, Piechulla B. *Plant Biochemistry*, 5th ed. Academic Press, 2021. Lipid chapter.
- **Primary literature:** the week-by-week Nakamura lab reading list in `plant-lipids-nakamura-reading-list.md` (required, supplementary, and presentation papers marked per week).
- **Selected reviews (posted on NTU COOL):**
  - Ohlrogge J, Browse J. Lipid biosynthesis. *Plant Cell* 7: 957–970 (1995).
  - Hölzl G, Dörmann P. Chloroplast lipids and their biosynthesis. *Annual Review of Plant Biology* 70: 51–81 (2019).
  - Nakamura Y. Plant phospholipid diversity: emerging functions in metabolism and protein–organelle interactions. *Trends in Plant Science* 22: 1027–1040 (2017).
  - Nakamura Y. Headgroup biosynthesis of phosphatidylcholine and phosphatidylethanolamine in seed plants. *Progress in Lipid Research* 82: 101091 (2021).
  - Nakamura Y. Phosphate starvation and membrane lipid remodeling in seed plants. *Progress in Lipid Research* 52: 43–50 (2013).
  - Nakamura Y, Ngo AH. Non-specific phospholipase C (NPC): an emerging class of phospholipase C in plant growth and development. *Journal of Plant Research* 133 (2020).
  - Bates PD, Stymne S, Ohlrogge J. Biochemical pathways in seed oil synthesis. *Current Opinion in Plant Biology* 16: 358–364 (2013).
  - Kanehara K, Yu C-Y, Cho Y. A lipid viewpoint on the plant endoplasmic reticulum stress response. *Journal of Experimental Botany* 73: 2835–2847 (2022).
  - Additional reviews on sphingolipids, sterols, and cutin and suberin will be added to NTU COOL before the relevant week.

- 大三下的專門課，是三門課裡最接近你專業的一門（Nakamura 實驗室背景、JXB 2022 lipid viewpoint）。可以直接用你的研究當案例，也最適合先做完投影片，建立三門課的製作節奏。
